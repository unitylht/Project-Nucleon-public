Hoang initially suggested treating payment later than the normal term as an approved extension. **17:42–19:09:** Hanting pointed out that lateness alone cannot prove approval. Hoang agreed to clarify approved extensions versus unapproved late payments.

| Category | Required evidence | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary credit sale | Applicable original terms and transaction | A ledger entry is not automatically an exception approval |
| Approved extension | An explicit confirmed decision linked to the request/invoice | Late payment alone is not approval |
| Unapproved late payment | Confirmed terms, matching/payment status and verified absence of an approved extension | An unlabelled case is not automatically unapproved |
| Unknown | Insufficient evidence | Preserve unknown instead of inventing yes/no |
| Refused request | Actual refusal record or reviewed/recalled case | It may never enter the sales ledger |

Analytical cautions added by the summariser, **not decisions made in the meeting**:

- A split containing only approvals cannot reveal how often refusals would be incorrectly approved. Label policy compliance separately from actual owner exception decisions.
- Missing similarity to approved examples does not itself prove refusal; any default-refusal rule needs explicit business authority and evaluation.
- Synthetic scenarios can demonstrate function, but cannot validate real-world accuracy. Keep independent test cases separate from examples used to design/tune the approach.
- Saving new cases, retrieving them and training model parameters are different methods. No method was selected by the informal word “train.”
