Status: design for the next Lab deployment on branch `explore/agent-coordinator-20261019`. The root site (`f95f50f`) and the preview (`df10ab6`) stay unchanged. Builds on the three earlier Lab designs in this folder. Team inputs: `docs/reviews/2026-10-21-team-meeting-and-model-review.md` (decisions, discussion, assumptions, evidence, the model review) and the two Tony documents already answered in `docs/reviews/2026-10-21-tony-questionnaire-response.md`.

## 1. Intent

The team agreed on 21 October that the Week 7 demonstration is one flow: import usable business records, understand a new payment-term extension request, obtain actual numerical results from a fixed, testable pipeline, interpret them with the owner's context, recommend, act within explicit authority or escalate, and retain the decision, its reasons and the feedback. The Lab today does the middle (understand, prepare, decide) on fictional data. The demonstrated gaps: the imported ledger is wrong (VND written as AUD with two decimals, no payment matched to an invoice, "still owed" unknown), retrieval fails after a reset and the failure reads as an empty queue, the import card stays actionable after use, old conversations survive a reset without a boundary, and there is no numerical result at all: the Lab "retrieves, it does not predict".

## 2. What the materials support (established, not assumed)

- **Team decisions.** Payment-term extensions only. A fixed numerical part that humans configure and the AI cannot change; the LLM interprets and flags but never changes the numbers. The staff ask the AI first; the AI acts only within authority the owner set; everything else escalates to the owner; every decision and disagreement is recorded. Single currency per dataset; conversion is the accountant's job. The AI proposes the import; a person checks and presses once.
- **Hoang's model.** It predicts a proxy, not the owner's decision: "Approve (proxy)" is a payment made while something was overdue and not refused; "Deny (proxy)" is three customers the owner named. 85 labelled payments, 8 denials from 3 customers, 2 holdout denials from one customer. Scores are class-weighted and uncalibrated. No artefact is saved, the shipped cleaner is behind the notebook, and the cleaned workbook is absent. The deterministic part (a FIFO ledger replay giving debt, overdue amount, days past due, prior late-receipt share, relationship length) is sound and portable to the stdlib server.
- **Therefore** the Lab shows numerical results that are facts computed from the records plus policy checks against limits the owner configured. It shows no probability, no confidence percentage and no "the model says approve". Where the meeting expected a confidence number, the product shows what it has: how many recorded decisions exist, how many for this customer, and which checks pass. The hook for a trained model is the feature set, computed with Hoang's names so his pipeline can plug in when it has labels that are decisions.

## 3. Decisions

### 3.1 Retrieval and failure honesty
1. The tool surface treats `current` as a hint. A `customerId` or `requestId` that no longer exists is dropped and the result carries `notes: ["The customer this conversation focused on is no longer in the records."]`; it is never a refusal. The Lab clears a focus the records reported gone.
2. A failed tool is never an empty result. The Lab replaces the model's reply by code when every tool call of the turn failed: "The records could not be read: <message>." When some failed, a code line is appended: "One check failed: <human label>: <message>." The prompt says the same, and the claim guard withholds "nothing/none/no items" claims about a tool that failed.
3. Reset boundaries. The records header carries `datasetId` (the `lastReset.at` or the creation time). Each Lab thread stores the `datasetId` it was created under. Threads from an earlier dataset are grouped in the sidebar under "Before the records reset on <date>", open read-only with a banner ("These records were replaced on <date>. Start a new conversation for the current records."), and their live-state cards show "not in the current records" instead of a status.

### 3.2 Import correctness
1. **Money unit.** `propose` detects the unit from the file: a currency column or header token (`VND`, `đ`, `AUD`, `$`), Vietnamese ledger headings (→ VND), otherwise unknown. The proposal view carries `unit: {detected, basis}` and `workspace: {currency, scale}`. Apply refuses a mismatch (409 `UNIT_MISMATCH`: "The file's amounts look like VND; these records are kept in AUD. Nothing was imported. Start a workspace in VND, or convert the file.") and the card says so with no Import button. An unknown unit imports as the workspace currency with a visible line "The file does not say its currency; amounts are taken as <currency>." A zero-scale currency never shows decimals anywhere (already true; test it on VND).
2. **Payment references.** The ledger split reads the invoice number a payment names in its description (`HĐ 73`, `hóa đơn 73`, `HD73`, `invoice 73`, `inv 73`, `số HĐ 73`; or an explicit reference column such as "Invoice Reference Mentioned") into `invoiceReference`; the existing allocation plan then matches it to that customer's invoice when the invoice exists and the payment is not larger than what it still owes. Unmatched payments stay unallocated. The card counts both: "73 of 116 payments name an invoice and will be matched; 43 stay unmatched and count toward the customer balance." A payment naming several invoices is flagged and unallocated. FIFO is never applied at import (allocation to an invoice the customer did not name is a guess).
3. **Still owed.** Per invoice: the stated amount if one was imported, else total minus allocations, with the basis in the text ("from allocated payments"). Per customer: invoiced minus received, which is exact, and the unmatched amount named as such ("VND 1,234,000 received, not matched to an invoice"). The text "no amount stated and no payments allocated" only appears when both are true and then names what would resolve it.
4. **Identity.** Customers created from a code column keep the code in the name ("Customer 0014") and the code as an alias so `findCustomers("0014")` and `findCustomers("BNSO")` (when the file has a name column) both find them. Invoice references are the original numbers.
5. **Import state.** The proposal is read live (`GET /api/records/imports/proposals/<id>`, staff and owner): `proposed`, `applied` (with batches and revert links), `refused` (unit mismatch) or `duplicate` (a proposal with the same file hash was already applied: "This file was imported on <date>: 137 invoices, 116 payments."). The card renders from the live state like prepared cards, so refresh, restart and deploy show the same thing; the Import button exists only for `proposed`. Pressing Import twice on one proposal returns the same batches (idempotent by proposal id); a second proposal of an already-imported file is `duplicate` and not applicable; a partially failed apply reverts its first batch and leaves the proposal `proposed` with the error on it.
6. **Workspace start.** The operator demo reset gains `to: 'empty'`: an empty workspace with no settings, so the owner sets the currency (VND, 0 decimals) on Setup before the first import. The shared Lab transitions this way, with backups and a dated note in `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`.

### 3.3 The numerical pipeline as a tool
1. `prototype/behaviour.py` (stdlib): `replay(customer, invoices, payments, allocations, opening, as_of)` reproduces the deterministic part of Hoang's pipeline with his feature names: `credit_term_days` (median of due minus issue over the customer's invoices; basis stated), `debt_before_m`, `overdue_before_m`, `max_days_past_due_before`, `prior_invoice_documents`, `prior_billed_m`, `prior_received_m`, `prior_receipt_count`, `has_prior_receipt`, `days_since_prior_receipt`, `observed_relationship_days`, `prior_late_receipt_fraction`. Recorded allocations are used where they exist; the rest is FIFO; `allocationBasis` says which (`recorded`, `fifo`, `mixed`). Every figure is money in the workspace unit or a day count, reconciled against the customer balance (`reconciles: true|false` with the difference).
2. Tool `assessRequest(requestId|'current')` (read; staff and owner): `{facts, policy, model, recommendation, evidence, limitations, subject}`.
   - `facts`: the features above, as numbers and as one sentence each ("VND 57,240,000 is overdue, the oldest 41 days past due. 3 of 8 earlier payments arrived while something was overdue.").
   - `policy`: the owner's authority settings and each check with pass/fail and a sentence: nothing overdue; no late history above the owner's tolerance; requested days within the staff limit; outstanding within the amount limit; policy default "no" when the owner set no limits.
   - `model`: `{status: 'unavailable', reason}` with the fixed reason text: "No approval model runs here. The team's labelled data are proxies for lateness, not owner decisions, and too few to measure (8 denials from 3 customers in the one ledger studied). The features above are computed with the same names so a model can be attached when real decisions exist."
   - `recommendation`: `{outcome: 'approve_within_authority' | 'escalate' | 'decline_by_policy', reasons: [...]}`, derived only from `policy`: all checks pass and a staff limit exists → `approve_within_authority`; a check the owner marked as a hard rule fails → `decline_by_policy`; otherwise `escalate`. Prediction, recommendation and authority are separate fields and are never merged into one word.
   - `evidence`: `{decisionsRecorded, decisionsForThisCustomer, similar: [{requestId, reference, askedFor, outcome, decidedOn, reason}]}` from recorded decisions of this customer (and the same customer's open concerns).
   - `limitations`: fixed sentences (allocation basis, no request-time model, data is one company and one year).
3. **Authority settings** (human-only; not on the tool surface for writing): `GET/PUT /api/setup/authority` (owner): `{staffMayApproveUpToDays: int|null, maxOutstanding: money|null, requireNothingOverdue: bool, maxPriorLateShare: number|null, hardRules: [names], maxConcernRounds: int (default 2)}`. Defaults: every limit null, `requireNothingOverdue` true, so everything escalates until the owner sets limits. The Lab shows an "Authority" panel for the owner; the agent reads the settings through `assessRequest.policy` and can propose a change in words, never make one.

### 3.4 Acting, escalating and retaining
1. `actWithinAuthority(requestId|'current')` (act, staff, confirmation): allowed only when the current assessment's recommendation is `approve_within_authority`; it records a decision `{outcome: 'approve', approvedDueDate: asked date, reason: the policy sentences, decidedBy: staff id, decisionSource: 'staff_within_authority', authority: the settings snapshot, assessment: the facts and checks}` and the event `decision.committed`. Otherwise 409 `NOT_WITHIN_AUTHORITY` with the failing check, and the case stays with the owner (the queue shows "escalated: <check>").
2. The owner's `decide` gains `decisionSource: 'owner'`, a snapshot of the latest assessment's recommendation and `followedRecommendation` (same outcome or not). `remember` gains `until` (date) so "for this customer until the end of the year" is one confirmed guidance line with an expiry; Tony's scope capture is this field: the Lab asks "Does that apply to this request only, or to this customer from now on (until when)?" only when the owner's words imply more than the one case.
3. Staff disagreement is `raiseConcern` (exists). A concern on a case with an assessment records `disagreesWith: recommendation`; the agent answers it from the facts (prompt rule: address the concern with the figures, never change them). After `maxConcernRounds` concerns the case is marked `escalated: 'concern rounds'` and waits for the owner. All of it is already in the case lines and events.
4. The decision card (Lab) and the case header show: outcome, by whom, the source (owner / staff within authority), whether it followed the recommendation, and the reason.

### 3.5 Presentation
1. One presentation per message. When a message has a primary or action card, the prose is at most two sentences; the Lab enforces it in code (keeps the first two sentences) and the prompt asks for it. Retained messages render exactly what live messages render (prose, one open card, folded Records and Activity); the `detail` field drives nothing else.
2. The assessment is a card (facts, checks with pass/fail, recommendation, evidence, "no model" line, limitations behind a fold) and the prose says what it means in one or two sentences.
3. Progress lines gain "Working out the numbers…" and "Recording the decision…".
4. Everything fits 390 px; Arial; no inline styles.

### 3.6 Scope captured from Tony's materials
Adopted: the scope question on an owner decision (3.4.2); the concern rounds (3.4.3); the owner's authority as explicit settings rather than inferred from one approval (3.3.3). Kept out: a standing rule inferred from a single approval; a positive relationship as permission; automatic decisions beyond the configured limits.

## 4. Non-goals and anti-goals
Non-goals: training or hosting Hoang's classifiers; credit-limit requests; multi-currency workspaces; customer communications; the root site or preview; a rotating owner questionnaire. Anti-goals: any invented confidence or probability; a tool failure shown as an empty list; silent currency relabelling; FIFO allocation presented as fact; a staff decision outside the configured limits; hiding the action card.

## 5. Validation

Lab tests: failed-tool reply rules; thread `datasetId` grouping; prose cap; assessment and authority flows with `FunctionModel`. Checks: `lab_check` (import card after reload shows applied; unit mismatch card; assessment card; authority panel; old-dataset group at 390 px), conversation scenarios (new request → assess → escalate; staff within authority; owner decides with a scope; concern rounds; failed tool honesty).
